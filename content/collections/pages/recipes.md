    title: 'Tarte Flambee With Piave Mornay'
    description: 'Alsace and Condrieu meet somewhere over an open flame, and the result is a tarte flambée that doesn’t know if it wants to be rustic or noble — so it’s both: golden, crackling dough draped in piave mornay, with grilled nectarine playing coy against salty prosciutto, while fresh thyme and a whisper of lemon zest referee the whole delicious argument. Paired with Viognier, because even a tarte deserves a wine equally at home in a vineyard and a fairy tale.'
    servings: '4'
    prep_time: 'approx 1 hour'
    image: images/vio2022.pairing.1.jpg
    ingredients: |-
      homemade tarte dough
      3 cups all purpose flour | plus more for dusting
      2 ¼ tsp. (1 packet) active dry yeast
      1 tsp granulated sugar
      1 tsp kosher salt
      1 cup warm water (105-110°F)
      2 TBSP extra virgin olive oil | plus more for brushing

      piave mornay sauce
      2 TBSP butter | unsalted
      2 TBSP all-purpose flour
      1 cup whole milk | warmed
      3/4 cup Piave Vecchio cheese | finely grated
      1 pinch kosher salt

      grilling nectarines & assebling the tartes for baking
      *Preheat the oven to 500°F for baking the fully assembled tartes. If you have a baking stone, place it inside while preheating for the best crispy crust.

      2 ripe but firm yellow nectarines | cut into 1/4-inch thick slices
      8 slices prosciutto | cut into 1-inch ribbons
      1 TBSP extra virgin olive oil | for grilling yellow nectarines
      2 tsp fresh thyme | leaves
      1 lemon | zested
      1 pinch flaky sea salt | to taste
    instructions: |-
      homemade tarte dough
      **Proof the yeast:** In a small bowl, combine 1 cups warm water (105–110°F), 1 teaspoons granulated sugar, and 2.3 teaspoons active dry yeast (about 1 packet). Let stand until foamy.
      **Mix the dough**: In a large bowl, whisk together 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting and 1 teaspoons kosher salt. Add the yeast mixture and 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for brushing. Mix until a shaggy dough forms.
      **Knead the dough:** Knead on a lightly floured surface until smooth and elastic (a stand mixer with a dough hook takes about 9 minutes).
      **First rise:** Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let rise until doubled in size.
      **Divide and rest:** Punch down the dough and divide into 4 equal portions. Shape each into a ball and let rest.
      **Shape the dough:** Roll each ball into an 8-inch circle, about 1/8th inch thick.

      _Store Bought Substitute: Trader Joe's Lavash Flatbread_

      piave mornay sauce
      Melt the butter: For the mornay sauce, melt 2 tablespoons unsalted butter in a saucepan over medium heat.
      Cook the roux: Whisk in 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour and cook until the raw flour smell cooks off and the roux has a lightly nutty aroma.
      Thicken the sauce: Slowly whisk in 1 cups whole milk, warmed until smooth. Simmer until thickened.
      Add the cheese: Remove from heat and stir in 3/4 cups finely grated Piave Vecchio cheese until silky.
      Season the sauce: Season with 1 pinch kosher salt, to taste. Keep warm.

      grilling nectarines & assebling the tartes for baking
      Grill the nectarines: Brush 2 ripe but firm nectarines, cut into thin 1/4-inch slices with 1 tablespoons olive oil, for grilling nectarines and grill over medium-high heat until lightly caramelized, about 4 minutes per side.
      Cook the flatbread crusts: Preheat a dry, ungreased cast iron pan or skillet over medium-high heat. Brush each rolled dough round lightly with olive oil and cook until puffed, with large air bubbles and light golden-brown spotting on both sides, about 2–5 minutes per side.
      Assemble the tartes: Spread each flatbread generously with the warm Piave Vecchio sauce. Arrange the grilled nectarines over the sauce, then drape the prosciutto ribbons evenly across the top.
      Bake the tartes: Transfer tartes onto the hot stone or a parchment-lined baking sheet and bake until warm and the prosciutto begins to lightly crisp, about 5-6 minutes.
    notes: |-
      to serve:
      Finish each tarte with lemon zest, fresh thyme leaves, and flaky sea salt, to taste. Serve immediately.
      Dough, sauce, and grilled nectarines can all be made ahead — assemble and bake just before serving. Preheating a baking stone or steel takes 30–45 minutes, so start the oven early in the process rather than after assembly.
